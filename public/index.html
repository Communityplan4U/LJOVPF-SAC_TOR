How Little Jamaica's advisory committee compares with other Toronto planning initiatives

Community in Public compared the Little Jamaica and Oakwood Vaughan Stakeholder Advisory Committee’s Terms of Reference (TOR) with ten other Toronto advisory bodies, using twelve criteria across five governance areas.

The results identify opportunities to strengthen community participation. All ten comparator TORs score higher overall, particularly in their written commitments to community influence over planning decisions, early participation, transparency and shared governance.

For years, community members have advised the City for community to have a meaningful role in shaping our future. This includes in 2026 through 4 deputations and 5 letters to the Planning and Housing Committee and a community petition in July, community members shared their priorities and recommendations with the Development Review Midtown team and sought the support of Mayor Olivia Chow and Councillors Josh Matlow, Alejandra Bravo, Mike Colle, and Frances Nunziata to ensure community has a meaningful voice in the planning of our neighbourhood.

This comparative evaluation and survey are independently developed and administered by Community in Public. They are not official City of Toronto or Stakeholder Advisory Committee engagement activities. A separate review will examine which commitments made to the community since 2016, in connection with the Eglinton LRT, have been fulfilled and which remain outstanding.
