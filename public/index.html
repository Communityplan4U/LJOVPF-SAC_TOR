The City's Terms of Reference for their Little Jamaica Framework is weakest, compared to similar and benchmark studies

Terms of Reference (TOR) describe how advisory committees work. Across twelve assessment criteria and ten comparable Toronto advisory bodies, Little Jamaica's SAC Terms of Reference score lowest overall, particularly in giving community members influence over planning priorities and recommendations, opportunities for early and meaningful participation, transparent records and accountability, and shared authority over committee governance. These weaknesses matter because residents, businesses and community organizations need more than opportunities to comment on City proposals: they need clear commitments that City staff and elected representatives will use these priorities to address displacement and support community wellbeing.

For years, including through the 2026 community letter to council in April and a community petition in July, community members already provided directions to Development Review Midtown team and sought the support of Mayor Olivia Chow and Councillors Josh Matlow, Alejandra Bravo and Mike Colle to ensure community has a meaningful voice in the planning of our neighbourhood. A seperate audit is being prepared to identify what of the commitments made to this community since 2016 in consideration of the Eglinton LRT has been implemented, and what is outstanding.

This comparative evaluation and survey are independently developed and administered by Community in Public. They are not official City of Toronto or Stakeholder Advisory Committee engagement activities.
